Rangers FC Leads Fan Experience Revolution

Three years after the Glasgow club bet on a new kind of digital matchday, supporters around the world are part of every game at Ibrox.

Sports business correspondent in Glasgow · 12 November 2029
Ibrox Stadium, Glasgow. There is a seat here for every supporter now, wherever they are.
On a matchday in Govan the ground is full long before kick-off, as it has been for generations. What has changed is everyone who is not inside it. In 2026 the club’s digital presence was one website and one app, the same for every fan. Today a supporter in Toronto, New York or a few streets from Edmiston Drive follows the build-up, the match and the aftermath in a way shaped to them, and in the company of the people they watch with. The club calls it social, immersive and personalized. Its rivals call it the standard to beat.

An empty seat at Ibrox Stadium symbolizes all the Rangers fans around the world who cannot attend a match.

Because there simply isn’t space.

But those millions of fans still get to be part of every Rangers FC Matchday through the club’s industry leading fan experience…

2026

In 2026, the Rangers were among the first professional sports franchises to invest in a new kind of fan experience.

The team partnered with Supporter Labs, an MIT startup, to build and scale the AI infrastructure for the social, immersive, and personalized experiences that its fans enjoy today.

Before

Prior to working with Supporter Labs, the Rangers’ digital presence was a one-size-fits-all fan experience with static, one-way communication.

One website.One app.One experience.

The Rangers website and app, 2026: news, fixtures, RangersTV, shop, tickets.

That digital fan experience provided information focused on upcoming games, team news, merchandise and tickets.

It lacked data and intelligence about team supporters and innovative products, services, and experiences tailored to their supporters.

2029

Today, in 2029, after working with Supporter Labs, the Rangers’ digital presence is all about engaging fans in dynamic, surprising ways.

It features two-way communication.
It offers content responsive to fan preferences, needs and behaviors.
It uses first-party data to constantly improve the fan experience.
It is social, immersive and personalized.
One Club.Many Fans.Many Experiences.

Rangers FC Membership Model

Fans take full advantage of their Rangers fan membership.

The Rangers were one of the first teams to build a fan membership business, building their MyGers supporters club into an industry powerhouse. It operates similar to the Amazon Prime membership model that anticipates the needs, wants and behaviors of its customers.

The Rangers FC membership creates a deeper connection between the Rangers and their fans. Through the membership, the Rangers establish a direct relationship with their fans and offer them a variety of products, services, and experiences, consistently delivering value above and beyond what fans expect.

EXPERIMENT 01

Matchday group chat

One home for a supporters’ group on matchday, with an agent that does the work for the group.

What supporters get
  • The news and alerts the group asks for
  • Plans handled: who is in, where, when
  • Answers about the match, a player or the rules when someone tags the agent
First version
  • A small number of supporters’ groups, one agent per chat
  • The agent posts only what the group asks for or a plan needs
  • Nothing booked or paid for without approval
What we measure
  • Groups still active after two weeks
  • Share of each group that joins
  • Plans made in the chat; how often the agent is tagged

EXPERIMENT 03

Pub finder

The right place to watch the Rangers, wherever a supporter is.

What supporters get
  • Supporters’ club bars first, then pubs showing the match with sound
  • How sure we are of each detail: confirmed, likely or unknown
  • A table booked after they approve, where the pub allows it
First version
  • Glasgow and one city abroad with an active supporters’ club, such as Toronto or New York
  • Each pub checked by hand; an honest “unknown” where we can’t confirm
  • After each match, supporters who went tell us what was true
What we measure
  • The “promise kept” rate: was the match on as listed, with sound when we said so?
  • Bookings made
  • Would they use it for the next match?
